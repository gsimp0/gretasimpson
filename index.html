§ 1About

I'm a PhD candidate at the University of Queensland, supervised by Associate Professor Jessica Taubert, with Dr Ashley York and Dr Lena Oestreich. My research currently uses ultra-high-field fMRI to ask how expressions of pain are processed in primary visual cortex, compared with expressions of fear.

Before my PhD I completed a Bachelor of Psychological Science (Honours) at UQ, where my Honours project asked whether pain expressions signal a threat to avoid or a need to approach. Using naturalistic face photographs, a behavioural odd-one-out task and 7T fMRI, I found that people place pain alongside fear and disgust, and that pain shows a right-lateralised response in the dorsal posterior superior temporal sulcus. This work is now under review.

§ 2Research

Fearful faces are thought to send feedback from higher brain areas down to V1, the first stage of visual processing in the cortex, arriving in its superficial layers. Pain expressions also signal threat, but they are more ambiguous: they can mean danger, or that someone needs help. My PhD asks whether pain recruits the same layer-specific feedback as fear, or whether it carries a distinct signature.

Pain and fear across the layers of V1 7T BOLD fMRI. Data collection underway.

Comparing responses to pain, fear and neutral faces in the superficial, middle and deep layers of V1.

Pain, fear and contrast sensitivity Behavioural psychophysics. In preparation.

Testing whether pain expressions change how well people detect faint visual contrast, as fear has been shown to.
